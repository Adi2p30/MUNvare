What is the UN


The United Nations stands as a global entity that offers a platform for its member nations to voice their perspectives and collaborate in taking meaningful actions. Comprising 193 Member States presently, the UN's operations are steered by the fundamental principles and goals enshrined in its founding Charter.


Throughout its existence, the UN has undergone transformative changes to align with the swiftly shifting global landscape. Yet, amidst these shifts, a constant remains: it remains the singular arena on Earth where all nations convene to address shared challenges and seek collective solutions that promote the welfare of all humanity.


The United Nations, or UN, represents an intergovernmental organization driven by a set of core objectives: upholding international peace and security, fostering amicable relations between nations, facilitating international cooperation, and serving as a focal point for coordinating the efforts of nations. It commands the distinction of being the largest and most recognized international body across the globe.

What are MUNs, Who Participates in one?
Model United Nations (MUN) is a simulation of the United Nations in which students can learn about diplomacy, international relations, and the United Nations. Participants typically assume the role of a delegate from a specific country and work to negotiate with other delegates on issues being discussed by the UN. Delegates seek to solve real-world problems using the policies and viewpoints of their allocated country.

To put it simply, MUNs are a unique type of debate competition.
where you represent a country and take on a topic or agenda making sure you respect the country's real life views and make solutions on the topic. MUNs are special because you get to research from the perspective of a country and you are forced to go along with those rules. It makes it a challenge and when you invest time in it, it's usually a very fun one.


Model United Nations conferences can be attended by students all around the world, as well as from the comfort of their own homes via online conferences. Universities, secondary schools, non-profit organizations, and other educational groups sponsor these conferences. Conferences are available at all academic levels, including college and university, high school, middle school, and elementary school. Model UN has no central governing organization; any school can form a Model UN team and register to attend any Model UN conference.


Executive Board: Executive Boards (EBs) in a Model United Nations (MUN) are the individuals responsible for overseeing the proceedings of the committee and ensuring that the rules and procedures of the MUN simulation are followed. They also are the ones to mark you during the committee

Unmoderated caucuses can be useful for allowing delegates to get a sense of the opinions and positions of other countries on an issue, but they may not be as productive as moderated caucuses in terms of coming to a consensus or making progress on a specific resolution.

A Crisis
 A crisis refers to a sudden and unexpected development related to the committee's agenda that can impact the proceedings of the session. In response to a crisis, the committee may conduct a moderated caucus or receive a presidential statement from the chair. However, participation in a crisis scenario requires a significant degree of knowledge and experience, as well as the ability to think critically and respond to questions and developments in real-time while representing a designated country. For these reasons, it is advisable that novice delegates refrain from participating in crisis scenarios until they have developed a sufficient level of understanding and proficiency in MUN.
 


Presidential Speeches 
Presidential Speeches simulate the addresses made by world leaders. While they are not a frequent occurrence in MUN committees, they can be a significant aspect of the proceedings. Presidential speeches typically do not have a set time limit and are guided by specific rules established by the delegate who is delivering the speech. The purpose of a presidential speech in MUN is to demonstrate decisive action, accountability, and a clear stance on an issue. These speeches can provide valuable insight into a country's position on the matter being discussed and help to shape the direction of the committee's discussions.

Majority 
Majority refers to the minimum number of votes required to pass a resolution or make a decision within a committee. The majority requirement may vary based on the rules of procedure or the type of committee. There are three types of majority 


1. Simple majority: A simple majority refers to more than half of the votes cast. For example, if there are 10 delegates in a committee and a resolution needs a simple majority to pass, it would need at least 6 votes in favor to pass.


2. Two-thirds majority: A two-thirds majority refers to two-thirds of the votes cast. For example, if there are 10 delegates in a committee and a resolution needs a two-thirds majority to pass, it would need at least 7 votes in favor to pass.


3. Absolute majority: An absolute majority refers to more than half of the total number of delegates in the committee, regardless of how many votes are cast. For example, if there are 10 delegates in a committee and a resolution needs an absolute majority to pass, it would need at least 6 votes in favor to pass, even if some delegates abstain from voting.


4. Consensus: Consensus refers to a situation in which all delegates agree on a resolution or motion. Consensus is often difficult to achieve, but it can be an effective way to build support for a resolution and ensure that all delegates are on board.

Chits 
Chits are a great way to make a point in a written format
Even though it might have less marking and may be forgotten. It sure has an impact.
A major function of chits is that they are used to ask questions. Questions that you didn’t have the chance to ask after a speech. Writing speeches is an effective strategy to make sure you are relevant in committee. 

There are two types of chits
1. Informal Chits
2. Formal chits


Formal chits are written communications during an MUN. 
In online events they can be sent to delegates via email for the purpose of asking questions or making substantive contributions to the discussions.


In the event that a delegate is unable to address a question in committee due to the limited opportunity for Points of Inquiry (POI), a formal chit can be used as an alternative means of communication. However, it is advisable to address questions in committee as it provides a platform for public debate and questioning, which can have a significant impact on the committee's discussions.


A substantial chit is a speech in the form of a chit. If you didn’t have time to speak in the moderated caucus a substantial chit is a great way to make up for that speech. Although it should be noted that substantial chits may not be evaluated as favorably as verbal contributions. Therefore, it is recommended that delegates prioritize speaking in committee over relying solely on substantial chits.


Informal chits, on the other hand, are used for non-substantive purposes, such as lobbying and discussing future moderated caucus topics. They can also be effective in establishing rapport with other delegates and building support for a particular position. Effective communication through informal chits can increase the likelihood that other delegates will be willing to join and support a delegate's cause.

Documentation


Documentation in the MUN is the final part of the MUN and it's the result of all the debate and discussion in the committee; they usually contain the solution brought up in committee to the agenda. It is a crucial part of the marking process.


There are three types of documentation that I have experienced in MUNs the past years.
Working Papers and Draft Resolution are one of the most popular documentations in MUNs; they are a formal representation of solutions from the delegates in blocs. 


Now let's get to the specifics


What is a resolution?
A formal document that contains all the issues and possibly all the sponsors and discussed solutions to them.
Sponsor
A sponsor or the author writes a resolution that is usually a delegate in the committee. Though this role is open to all, sometimes observer countries aren't allowed to write or draft a resolution.
Being a sponsor does mean that you need the other delegates voting in your resolution to agree on you being the author to that resolution.
Usually there are 2-3 authors but this changes from conference to conference.


Resolutions are written in the second half of the committee and they are written in an unmoderated caucus where the chair will usually allow the delegates to take an unmoderated caucus. Conferences require you to talk about what you have written in the resolution like solutions and issues before you write the resolution in committee and in a moderated debate.
Signatories 
Signatories are the countries that have signed a resolution or treaty. In the context of an MUN conference, signatories are the countries that have formally agreed to support and be bound by the provisions of a resolution or treaty.
During an MUN conference, delegates may work to draft and negotiate resolutions on various issues being discussed by the UN. Once a resolution has been finalized, delegates may choose to sign it in order to show their support and commitment to its provisions. The countries that sign the resolution are known as the signatories.


It is important for delegates to be aware of the signatories to a resolution or treaty, as this can help them understand the level of support for the resolution and the potential impact it may have. Signatories may also be expected to take action to implement the provisions of the resolution or treaty within their own countries.


Format of the Draft Resolution
Though this might change from chair to chair this is one of the common formats of a draft resolution 


1. The header 
It consists of the Committee name, Sponsors, Signatories and the Agenda 


Most conferences require a minimum number or percentage of sponsors and signatories (or a combination of both) before a resolution can be presented; this requires you to show your lobbying or convincing skills in committee.


2. Preambulatory clauses
THe preambles state all the issues that the committees want to solve and it also states the past solutions and actions taken on this issue. Preamble Clauses usually include:


1. Past UN resolutions, treaties, or conventions related to the topic
2. Past regional, non-governmental, or national efforts in resolving this topic
3. References to the UN Charter or other international frameworks and laws
4. Statements made by the Secretary-General or a relevant UN body or agency


General background info formation or facts about the topic, its significance, and its impact.


To write a preambulatory clause first 
Look for the content of the clause which is mentioned in the points above.
After you find the content use a preambulatory phrase from the list below whichever one feels most appropriate for your point. Make sure you add a comma at the end of your clause.


3. Operative clauses
Operative clauses are the solutions to the problems that you have mentioned in the preambulatory clauses. 


To write an operative clause. First, take a solution that you want to include in the draft resolution. Then add an operative phrase (mentioned in the lists below) and end it with a semicolon. In case it's the last operative clause make sure you add a period. Operative clauses must be numbered.

  Amendments
Amendments are proposed changes to a resolution or treaty. Amendments can be proposed by any delegate and are typically used to make changes to the language or provisions of a resolution or treaty in order to improve it or make it more acceptable to a wider group of countries.


During an MUN conference, delegates may work to draft and negotiate resolutions on various issues being discussed by the UN. Once a resolution has been finalized, other delegates may choose to propose amendments to the resolution in order to make changes to its language or provisions.


Amendments can be useful for improving the clarity or effectiveness of a resolution, but they can also be a source of contention if delegates disagree on the proposed changes. It is important for delegates to carefully consider the impact of any proposed amendments and to try to reach a compromise if necessary.


Unfriendly Amendments
As the name suggests these are amendments that don't require the agreement of the sponsors to pass. Though if the sponsors accept the amendments it doesnt need to be voted on. But if the sponsors don't accept then there will be a voting where the majority required to accept the amendment will be two thirds of the committee. 
 
Friendly Amendments
Friendly amendments are those that receive approval from every sponsor of the document. If all the sponsors agree then there is no need for a vote. Such amendments are called friendly amendments.
These amendments could be grammatical errors in the document. 


 
It is important to remember that preambulatory clauses cannot be amended


Non grammatical Amendments show deep understanding of the topic and show the character of the delegate; it shows them the standards you keep as a delegate and the quality of research you have.

Voting Procedure


The voting procedure is the final result that decides whether documentation made in the committee passes or no documentation if the committee has failed.
In draft resolutions special majority is required to pass a resolution. Special majority is 2/3rds of the committee in favor.


During voting procedure only members of the committees are allowed to vote observers are usually not allowed to vote in committees that might have some observers.
They are also not allowed to be there during the voting procedure; they are usually let out of the committee. 


The most formal session will always be voting during voting 
1. No lobbying crosstalk or chit sending will be allowed
2. Though points are allowed during voting procedure they are discouraged.
3. The doors of the committee will be closed.


There are generally 3 rounds of voting. But if all the delegates vote before the start of the third round. A third round may be skipped.
The possible responses during voting procedure
1. Yes: a vote for passing the document


2. No: a vote against the document


3. Yes, with Rights: A vote for passing the document that allows the delegate to explain their vote. Though they are usually used if a delegate votes against their foreign policy.


4. No, with Rights: a vote against the document that allows the delegate to explain their vote. Though they are usually used if a delegate votes against their foreign policy.


5. Abstain: A vote that shows neither agreement or disagreement. This vote does not count in the total votes.


6. Pass: This allows you to pass the opportunity of voting for a later round. A pass cannot be used in the third round. Typically used to see the votes of other delegates. But you will lose your right to abstain if you decide to pass.


These rules may be changed by the chair at their discretion

Attire
Understanding the dress code is crucial to making a good impression and feeling confident during the event. In India, the dress code on the first day of the conference is usually western formal wear, such as suits or blazers for men. Women often opt for western formals as well, which may include dresses or pantsuits. On the second day, however, the dress code changes to Indian formals, with men wearing kurtas and women wearing elegant sarees. It's important to note that dress codes may vary depending on the MUN conference and the country it's taking place in, so it's always a good idea to do some research beforehand to ensure you're dressed appropriately. In addition, delegates should ensure that their clothing is comfortable enough to allow them to concentrate on the conference and not be distracted by discomfort.




Offline vs Online MUNs
There are two distinct modes of Model United Nations (MUNs): offline and online. While I personally prefer the offline format, which involves the traditional practice of writing chits and participating in live committee debates, there's a growing recognition that the online format offers greater accessibility and ease of hosting.


Participating in offline MUNs is an invaluable experience. It offers a tangible sense of how MUNs are conducted, with delegates physically present and interacting face-to-face in a committee setting. The offline format immerses participants in the full MUN atmosphere, encompassing everything that a MUN embodies. From the dress code, which encourages a heightened sense of engagement, to the palpable feeling of being in an attentive committee, offline MUNs provide a full understanding of the dynamics of MUN.


For newcomers to MUN, I strongly recommend starting with offline MUNs. The authenticity of the experience, along with the sensory engagement it offers, is an exceptional learning opportunity. However, it's worth acknowledging that the online format has its advantages in terms of accessibility and convenience. As the MUN landscape evolves, both formats contribute to the MUN experience in different ways.
